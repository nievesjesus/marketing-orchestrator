# Check-in report

A short, plain report on how a campaign or a period of posts went (SKILL.md section 11). Write it in the user's language, with everyday words. **Every number comes from the user, Postiz's analytics screen or their own answers: never invent or estimate figures.**

## 1. Get the numbers

The Postiz connector has **no analytics tool**, so:

1. Ask the user to open **Postiz → Analytics** (or each network's own statistics page) and paste the numbers or describe them, for the period and, if they have it, for the period before.
2. If they can't or don't want to, ask **3 simple questions** instead:
   - What came in during these weeks? (messages, bookings, orders, visits: whatever the goal was)
   - Which post got the most reaction, and which got almost none?
   - Roughly how many followers did you have before and now?
3. Use `postsListTool` for the period to list **what was posted when** (network, date, first line), so each number can be tied to a post.

Say plainly where each figure comes from ("from your Postiz screen", "from your answers"). If something is missing, write "not known" in the table; don't fill the gap.

## 2. Plain metric names

| Say | Instead of | Means |
|---|---|---|
| People who saw it | reach, impressions | How many people the post was shown to |
| Reactions | engagement | Likes, comments, saves, shares added up |
| Link taps | clicks, CTR | People who tapped the link |
| New followers | follower growth | Followers gained in the period |
| Messages, bookings, orders | conversions | Whatever the campaign goal counted |

## 3. The report (in this order)

**Summary (2–3 lines):** how it went against the goal, the one thing that worked, the one thing that didn't.

**Numbers table:**

| What we watched | This period | Before | Change | Goal | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| <the campaign's main number> | <n> | <n or not known> | <up, down or same, with the amount> | <the goal they set, or "none set"> | On track / Needs attention / Off track |

Status words: **On track** (at or above the goal), **Needs attention** (below the goal but close, or no goal to compare with), **Off track** (clearly below). Three to five rows are enough: the campaign's number first, then people who saw it, reactions, link taps, new followers.

**Best 3 and weakest 2 posts:** for each, the date, network, first line, the number that stands out, and a likely reason, written as a **guess** ("probably because the photo was your own, and it went out at lunchtime"). Compare hooks, visuals (own photo, generated, composed), formats, networks and times.

**What to do first, what to do later:** at most 3 items each, in plain sentences. "First" means big effect for little effort; "later" means more effort or a smaller effect.

**One test for next period:** one change, how you'll know it worked, and how long to run it (for example "post the same kind of offer with your own photo instead of a graphic, on both Tuesdays, and compare saves").

## 4. Rules

- **Small samples:** with only a few posts (as a rough rule, fewer than 6 in the period or on a network) say **"too few posts to be sure"** and treat differences as hints, not proof.
- No jargon (no CTR, ROAS, attribution). No numbers the user didn't give you. No promises about what will happen next.
- Never blame the user. Keep the tone encouraging and practical.
- If everything is off track, say it kindly and pick the single most useful next step.

## 5. After the report

- Offer to save what worked (hooks, visuals, times) in the brand card's **Learned** section.
- Offer the next step: a new campaign, a repeat of the best post type, or the test above.
